### 1. Market discovery (Gamma API, `gamma-api.polymarket.com`)

| Length | Slug pattern | Example | Created ahead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5m | `btc-updown-5m-<unix_start>` | `btc-updown-5m-1794417900` | ~24 h |

| 1h | `bitcoin-up-or-down-<month>-<day>-<year>-<hour><am\|pm>-et` | `bitcoin-up-or-down-november-11-2026-12pm-et` | ~48 h |

- **Differs from brief:** the hourly slug now contains the year. The year-less slug still resolves,
  but to the 2025 market of the same date. Do not build hourly slugs without the year.
- Safer hourly discovery: `GET /events?series_id=10114&closed=false` (series `btc-up-or-down-hourly`)
  returns all ~49 open hourly events. Series ids: 5m = 10684, 15m = 10192, 1h = 10114.

- Each market has two tokens, `clobTokenIds = [Up, Down]`.

### 2. Rules and settlement

Rules text, 5m and 15m (identical):

> This market will resolve to "Up" if the time-weighted average price (TWAP) of Bitcoin, generated by
> Chainlink, of the time range specified in the title is greater than or equal to the price at the
> beginning of that range. Otherwise, it will resolve to "Down".
> The resolution source for this market is information from Chainlink, specifically the BTC/USD TWAP
> data stream available at https://data.chain.link/streams/btc-usd-twap-60s-streams.
> Please note that this market is about the price according to the TWAP Chainlink data stream for the
> asset pair BTC/USD, not according to any other sources or spot markets.

Rules text, 1h:

> This market will resolve to "Up" if the close price is greater than or equal to the open price for
> the BTC/USDT 1 hour candle that begins on the time and date specified in the title. Otherwise, this
> market will resolve to "Down".
> The resolution source for this market is information from Binance, specifically the BTC/USDT pair
> (https://www.binance.com/en/trade/BTC_USDT). The close "C" and open "O" displayed at the top of the
> graph for the relevant "1H" candle will be used once the data for that candle is finalized.
> Please note that this market is about the price according to Binance BTC/USDT, not according to
> other exchanges or trading pairs.

| Length | Source | Line (open) | Close | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5m | Chainlink BTC/USD **60s** TWAP stream | TWAP value stamped exactly at window open T0 | TWAP value stamped exactly at close T1 | automatic, ~80 s after close |
| 15m | Chainlink BTC/USD 60s TWAP stream | same | same | automatic, ~80 s after close |
| 1h | Binance BTC/USDT 1h candle | candle open (first trade of the hour) | candle close (last trade before hh:59:59.999) | UMA, ~11 min after close |

- **Differs from brief:** the 5m market settles on the **60-second** average, not 30 seconds
  (`cryptoMarketConfig: {"id": "btc-5m-twap-60", "twapLookbackSeconds": 60}`). 5m and 15m use the same stream.
  The averaging adjustment in Phase 2 should use w = 60 for both.
- Alignment, measured: the TWAP stream value with source timestamp T is a trailing average, so the line
  covers roughly (T0 − 60 s, T0] and the close covers (T1 − 60 s, T1]. Our recorded value at the exact
  boundary second matched Gamma's `priceToBeat` to all printed digits (difference 0.0000) at
  17:35, 17:40, 17:45 (5m) and 17:45 (15m). One second either side was off by $0.04–$0.73.
- A window's close equals the next window's line (same number), and a 15m line equals the 5m line at the same instant.
- The TWAP cannot be rebuilt from the 1-second Chainlink spot stream: averaging 60 spot samples misses
  it by up to $4. The TWAP stream itself must be recorded.
- 1h check: Gamma `priceToBeat` 73991.97 / `finalPrice` 74260.07 for the 11AM ET market equal the
  Binance 16:00 UTC 1h candle open / close exactly.

### 3. CLOB market websocket

`wss://ws-subscriptions-clob.polymarket.com/ws/market` — confirmed, public, no credentials.

- Subscribe: `{"assets_ids": [...], "type": "market", "custom_feature_enabled": true}`.
  Add/remove later with `{"assets_ids": [...], "operation": "subscribe"|"unsubscribe"}`.
- Heartbeat: send text `PING` every 10 s, server answers `PONG`.
- Message types (`event_type`): `book` (full snapshot, all levels), `price_change` (level deltas, each
  carrying `best_bid`/`best_ask`), `last_trade_price`, `tick_size_change`; with the custom flag also
  `best_bid_ask`, `new_market`, `market_resolved`. `timestamp` is epoch ms as a string.
- Measured rates over 30 s (per market, both tokens):

| Market | price_change /s | book /s | best_bid_ask /s | raw JSON |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| live 5m | 922 | 17 | 20 | 640 KB/s |
| live 15m | 378 | 1.2 | 3 | 240 KB/s |
| live 1h | 78 | 0.3 | 1.2 | 50 KB/s |
| next 5m (not yet open) | 30 | 0.1 | 0.1 | 19 KB/s |
| 5m opening in 6 h | 10 | 0.1 | 0 | 6 KB/s |

  About 1,400 updates/s on the three live contracts, roughly 120 million per day. Top-of-book changes
  are only about 25/s; nearly all updates are deeper in the book.

- **Differs from brief:** there is no 30s stream, and none is needed since no market in scope uses it.

- **Risk:** the docs say the legacy RTDS price topics are "planned for removal one month after" SDK
  0.11.0, which was released 2026-09-23. That date has passed. The feed still works but can vanish
  without notice. The replacement needs CLOB API credentials, which conflicts with "no trading keys".

### 5. Tick size and price to beat

- Tick size: `orderPriceMinTickSize` on the Gamma market, 0.01 for all three lengths while live.
  It is not constant: resolved markets show 0.001, and the websocket emits `tick_size_change`

- Price to beat: exposed as `eventMetadata.priceToBeat` on the Gamma **event**, with `finalPrice` after close.

### What differs from the brief or needs attention

- **The Chainlink feed itself skips about 1% of seconds.** The same 105 seconds are absent from both

### Two additions to the brief's rules, both from what the data showed

1. **Staleness term in the error bar (on by default).** With the brief's bars (tick and line error
   only, and our line error is zero) the model signalled on 61% of seconds with two or more votes.
   The bars leave out that our spot price is old: the Chainlink price is on average 2.1 s old when
   we use it and moves about $5 in that time (measured sd $5.06, Binance $3.38). The code now adds
   spot x realised vol x sqrt(age) to the line error, which matches the measured $5. Signals drop
   to 12% of seconds. `python backtest.py --brief-bars` gives the brief's original bars.
2. **A signal must hold 3 seconds before it is traded.** 41% of tradable signals last one second and
   25% two. Those are the market moving before our feed does: the contract reprices, our spot
   follows a second or two later, and the "mispricing" is gone. The first trades the backtest took
   were of this kind. The fill is at the next second's quotes and only if the edge is still there.

### Other choices where the brief was silent

- **Which nu.** Legs that share an expiry are only live together for the last 5 minutes (15 minutes
  for 15m + 1h), so the time left sets the horizon, not the contract length. The code uses the nu of
  the shortest fitted horizon that covers the time left. The 60-minute nu is used only in the
  calibration of hourly contracts.
- **Events are longer than "the last 5 minutes".** At a full hour the 15m and 1h legs share the
  expiry for 15 minutes, so those events run 900 seconds: 2 legs for 10 minutes, then 3.
- **Windows for the tail fit** are clock-aligned and non-overlapping, like the contracts.
- **As-of joins use receive time**, so a value counts only once it reached us.
- **Last 60 seconds of a 5m/15m leg** (time left <= averaging window): the part of the average
  already seen is treated as fixed, the reference price is the blend of that part and spot, and the
  effective time left is tau^3 / (3 w^2). It joins tau - 2w/3 smoothly at tau = w.
- **A leg whose price is on the wrong side of 0.5** for where spot is (no positive width fits) does
  not vote.
- **Two legs:** the range is valued twice, once at each leg's own width. It is traded only if both
  values are on the same side of the executable price; the smaller edge counts. The anchor is not used.
- **Range trade when the outlier is the strike nearest to spot:** its neighbour is the next strike
  across spot. If there is none, no trade.
- **Execution** uses the Up books only: buying the range pays the lower strike's ask and receives
  the higher strike's bid. No fees, no size limit.
- **Threshold.** The backtest keeps the brief's starting value (4.5 cents) until the first part of
  the data has at least 30 trades to choose from.
- **"Quiet period"** for the false-alarm rate is the third of seconds with the lowest realised vol.
- **Plots** are written as SVG files by a 70-line helper (`svgplot.py`), because matplotlib is not
  on the allowed list. QQ plots and the calibration plot are in `C:\polydata\analysis`.

### Findings so far (40 events; far too few to judge the strategy)

- **No P&L estimate yet.** With both rules on, the reported part has 1 trade. A single range trade
  wins or loses tens of cents on an edge of 5 to 10, so about 100 trades are needed before the
  average means anything. At the current rate that is roughly the planned two weeks.
- **Prices have not moved toward the model after a signal.** This test is far less noisy than P&L
  at expiry: every signal that held 3 s is entered and looked at again 30 s later. Over 32 signals in
  8 events the range's mid moved +1.6 cents in the signal's direction (standard error 1.2) against a
  model edge of 3.0, and a round trip at bid and ask lost 0.6 cents. For the 6 signals above 4.5
  cents the move was -1.5 (se 2.0). So far there is no evidence on real data that the signals pick
  out mispricings rather than differences the model does not capture. The backtest reports this
  test in section 2 on every run.
- **The error bars are the right size for ordinary seconds.** Across 8,389 seconds where two legs
  vote, the difference between their votes has a robust spread of 0.98 error bars (1.0 is ideal),
  and each pair is close to that (0.80 to 1.01). The measuring layer therefore works on real data.
  But 11% of seconds lie beyond 2.5 bars where noise alone would give about 1%. Those are the
  signals, and the open question is whether they are mispricings or a fat tail of ordinary noise.
  The convergence test above is what will settle it. Reported in section 3 of the backtest.
- **Implied widths against realised vol.** The market's implied width is typically 1.0 to 1.2 times
  the realised-vol anchor. Far-from-the-money prices imply a narrower width (0.87 times beyond
  |q| = 2.5), so the market's tails look thinner than the fitted t, not fatter.
- **Three-leg seconds are rare:** 405 of 36,000 event seconds, in 4 of 40 events. The hourly contract
  is usually outside [0.02, 0.98] by its last minutes. Most signals are two-leg ones.
- **A "range" across feeds is not a true range.** Binance BTC/USDT was on average $91 above
  Chainlink BTC/USD (sd $8). The 1h leg settles on Binance, the others on Chainlink, so a 15m/1h
  pair can pay -1 as well as 0 or +1. Strikes are compared as ln(line / spot) on each leg's own feed.
- **Realised-vol anchor.** The fitted t scale for 5-minute returns is 0.80; the brief's formula
  sqrt((nu-2)/nu) gives 0.70. Realised vol over the previous hour understates the next window's
  spread by about 15%, inside the 25% error bar.
- **Calibration** (width from realised vol only, 139 markets): Brier 0.138 normal, 0.140 t. No
  visible advantage for the t so far.
- **Monotonicity is not a trade at our speed.** 5m vs 15m are out of order at executable prices on
  25 seconds in 3 events (mean credit 2.6 cents), 20 of them in one event where the two lines were
  almost equal. One second later the credit was still there on 7 of the 25 and averaged -0.4 cents.
  None against the 1h leg.
- **The line can be missing.** The Chainlink feed skipped the second 00:25:00 on 2026-11-12, so one
  5m window had no line and the one before it no closing value in our data. Such legs do not vote.
- **Polymarket's public price history cannot stand in for recorded books.** `clob.polymarket.com/
  prices-history` returns about one price a minute for expired contracts, but compared with our
  recorded books at the same timestamps it was off by up to 15 cents in moving markets (0.595
  against a recorded 0.45 / 0.46). A longer backtest on that data would be meaningless, so the
  result has to wait for our own recording.
- **Adjusting the stale Chainlink price with Binance's latest move** would cut its error from $5.1
  to $3.2. Not built: the brief says each leg uses its own feed, and the gain is modest.

### Update 2026-11-12 10:40 UTC (57 events, 14 hours): a fix, and the rules are now frozen

By mid-morning the error bars had drifted too tight (contracts disagreed by 1.17 bars in the
typical second, signals on 18.5% of seconds, 26% between 07:00 and 08:00 UTC). Two causes, both in
the staleness term:

1. **Volatility was taken from the previous 60 minutes only.** When the market wakes up, that
   figure lags, so the allowance for our stale spot price was too small. The term now uses the
   larger of the 60-minute figure and the last 5 minutes (30 ten-second returns).
2. **After the two reboots the 60-minute figure was missing**, and the code then added no staleness
   allowance at all. A leg now does not vote when no volatility figure exists.

After the fix, on the same data: contracts disagree by 0.96 error bars in the typical second,
signals on 6.9% of seconds, and after a lasting signal the price moved +2.4 cents toward the model
in 30 seconds (standard error 1.2) against an edge of 3.8. That is the first sign of signals that
predict anything, but it rests on 25 signals in 6 events and was measured on data the fix was
designed on. Held-to-expiry: 3 trades, all losers.

**The rules are frozen as of this update.** Data recorded after 2026-11-12 11:00 UTC is the honest
test: `python backtest.py 2026-11-12T11:00 2026-11-30`.

Still unexplained: the 15m contract implies a width 15 to 20% wider than the 1h contract in every
hourly event, and more when the market is quiet (one event at 09:00 UTC produced most of the
remaining false signals). A fixed per-contract correction fitted on the first part of the data did
not carry over to the second part (the 1h offset went from 0.84 to 1.14), so none was added.

### Disagreement limit scan (2026-11-12 11:10 UTC, 59 events)

The 2.5 error-bar limit was meant to fire on about 1% of seconds but fires on about 6%, because
disagreement between contracts has a fat tail (the median is right, the 99th percentile is 3.8
bars where a normal gives 2.6). The backtest now evaluates stricter limits alongside the frozen
2.5 baseline (section 4) and picks the lowest one that signals on at most 1% of seconds in the
first part of the data. The choice uses signal counts only, never P&L.

| Limit | Seconds with a signal (first / second part) | Lasting signals | In events | Price move after 30 s | Trades (second part) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 | 5.7% / 7.8% | 31 | 7 | +1.0 c (se 1.2) | 2 |
| 3.0 | 3.0% / 4.4% | 18 | 5 | -1.9 c (se 1.4) | 1 |
| 3.5 | 1.4% / 2.1% | 6 | 4 | -0.3 c (se 2.4) | 0 |
| 4.0 | 0.7% / 1.0% | 2 | 2 | too few | 0 |
| 5.0 | 0.1% / 0.3% | 1 | 1 | too few | 0 |

The chosen limit is 4.0. At that setting almost nothing is left: 2 lasting signals in 59 events,
about one a day. The stricter limits do not pick out better signals either; the price move after a
signal is no better at 3.0 or 3.5 than at 2.5. Large disagreements that last are rare, and the ones
seen so far were not followed by the market moving toward the model.

### How the 15m and 1h contracts differ (2026-11-12, 4,840 seconds in 10 hourly events)

- **Size:** the 15m is priced a median 1.6 cents closer to 0.50 than the 1h's implied width says it
  should be (middle half of seconds: 0.1 to 3.8 cents). Seen from the other side, the 1h is 1.9 cents
  further from 0.50 than the 15m implies. In width terms the 15m implies 1.15 times the 1h's width.
- **Same direction against the 5m:** the 15m also implies a width 6% wider than the 5m, which shares
  its feed and its averaging. Ordering of implied widths: 15m > 5m > 1h.
- **The averaging adjustment explains part:** without the tau - 2w/3 adjustment for the Chainlink
  legs the 15m/1h ratio falls from 1.15 to 1.09. The market may not price that adjustment.
- **Tail shape does not explain it:** with nu = 6, 10, 20 or a normal distribution the median ratio
  stays between 1.10 and 1.12. A thinner tail only moves the bias from the seconds where the 1h is
  further from 0.50 to the seconds where the 15m is.
- **Not explained:** the remaining 4 to 10%. It is about the size of one tick plus the spread.

| bias_corrected | 23 (10) | -3.7 c (se 1.5) vs 5.8 c | -6.9 c (se 2.1) | 6, +11 c (-31 to +53) |

| exit_30s (sell after 30 s) | 32 (8) | as baseline | as baseline | 5, -4 c (-13 to +5) |

What it shows:

- **No variant's signals are followed by the price moving toward the model.** The clearest case is
  `brief_bars`: 559 lasting signals in 64 events and a move of 0.0 cents (standard error 0.4)
  against 2.5 cents predicted. After the freeze every variant's move is zero or negative.
- **`shared_stale` is the only one with a positive move** (+1.3 c, about two standard errors, on all
  data), but a third of the predicted edge, and it is negative after the freeze.
- **The positive P&L of the bias variants is noise.** Their price move is negative, and the
  intervals span tens of cents either side.
- **Selling after 30 seconds loses about the spread** (-4 c per trade), as the price move predicts.
- **The 30-second test measures convergence, not value at expiry.** A mispricing that persists
  until settlement would not show in it. The only variant with enough resolved trades to say
  anything about expiry, `brief_bars` (29), lost 13 cents per trade (-30 to +5).

## Phase 3: the July recording (2026-11-12, 21:00 to 22:30 UTC)

A teammate shared a recording of the same contracts from July 2026 (`README.md`, "Shared dataset").
It has ten to twenty times the shared expiries of our own recording, so the frozen rules were run on
it unchanged. Code: `july.py` (builds the per-second tables), `evidence.py` (the tests),
`scripts/fetch_july_reference.py` (public reference data). Full outputs are in
`C:\polydata\reports\evidence_*.txt`.

### What the July data is, and how it differs from ours

| | Our recording (November) | July tick extracts | July polls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared expiries | 104 so far | 1,056 (07-11 18:30 to 07-22 19:15) | 1,996 (07-02 to 07-22), 940 of them without tick data |
| Quotes | every book update | every book update | best bid and ask about every 3 s |
| Settlement of 5m and 15m | Chainlink 60-second average | **Chainlink spot price at the window's ends** | same as tick |
| Chainlink feed | recorded | **not recorded** | not recorded |
| Lines and outcomes | recorded, and official | official (Polymarket's Gamma API) | official |
| Clock | within 0.6 s | 9 to 21 s fast, measured per file to about 10 ms and removed | same clock, known to about 1 s |

- **Settlement rule.** The rules text of every July 5m and 15m contract names the Chainlink BTC/USD
  stream, with no average. So for July the averaging window is 0 for all three contracts. This is a
  different product from today's in one respect that matters to the model.
- **No Chainlink price.** The official line of each 5m contract is the Chainlink price at a
  5-minute boundary, so the gap between Binance and Chainlink is known exactly every 5 minutes (July
  mean $53, sd $11). Between boundaries Chainlink is taken as Binance minus the last known gap.
  Checked on our November data, where both exist: the gap wobbles by about $5 (robust $3.4) between
  two times 5 minutes apart and by the same amount 15 minutes apart, so it does not drift. $5 is what
  our live Chainlink price is off by through being 2 s old, so the stand-in is given an age of 3 s
  in the error bars (2 s for this, 1 s for Binance's own delay). In July the gap between consecutive
  boundaries also changed by $5.0 (robust $3.8).
- **Is the stand-in good enough?** The check is the same as for our own data: across 160,000
  seconds with two votes, contracts disagree by 1.01 error bars in the typical second (5m vs 15m
  0.85, 15m vs 1h 1.26). The error bars are the right size on average.
- **Official data is consistent.** For all 7,852 July contracts with a published line and final
  price (of 8,485), the outcome equals "final price at or above the line". For the 1h contracts, line and final price equal the Binance candle's open
  and close to the cent.
- **Fees.** Polymarket charges takers 0.07 x p x (1 - p) per share on these contracts (1.75 cents
  at a price of 0.50), confirmed in its fee documentation and in the contracts' own settings. The
  range trade has two legs; the fee averaged 2.2 cents per trade. All P&L is now reported before and
  after it (`FEE_RATE` in `backtest.py`). The earlier sections of this file are before fees.

### Result: does the other contract's width carry information? (no thresholds, no trade rules)

For a contract priced p, let m be the model's probability at the other contract's width. If the
disagreement means anything, the contract should finish Up more often than p says when m is above
p. `evidence.py` regresses (outcome - p) on (m - p), clustered by expiry. A slope of 1 means the
other contract was right, 0 means the contract's own price was.

| Contract | Width from | Seconds | Slope on signal seconds, July first delivery | Design half / confirm half |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5m | 15m | 5,982 in 296 expiries | 0.36 (se 0.20) | 0.15 / 0.43 |
| 15m | 5m | 5,982 in 296 expiries | 0.41 (se 0.19) | 0.41 / 0.42 |
| 15m | 1h | 8,250 in 125 expiries | -0.17 (se 0.34) | -0.05 / -0.22 |
| 1h | 15m | 8,250 in 125 expiries | 0.35 (se 0.26) | 0.54 / 0.25 |

- For the 5m and 15m pair each contract's width explains about 40% of the other's gap, in both
  halves. That is what two noisy prices of the same thing look like: the truth is in between, and
  the range trade (which needs both views to agree on the direction) picks that up.
- Over all seconds, not only signal seconds, the four slopes are 0.05 to 0.15 and within two
  standard errors of zero. The information is in the large disagreements.
- On our November data the same two slopes are 0.86 (se 0.77) and 0.66 (se 0.38): the same sign,
  far too noisy to count.
- Prices close only 4 to 13% of the gap within 60 seconds.

### Why November may differ, and the two candidate rules

- Under today's rule the 15m implies a width 13% wider than the 1h (in 18 of 21 hourly events). In
  July the ratio was 0.99. Something about the 60-second average is priced differently from our
  adjustment (time left minus 40 s), or the two feeds are compared less cleanly than in July.
- Four of the six November trades had the 1h as one leg, and all four lost.
- Two rule sets were therefore defined before the second delivery was pulled, and are tracked from
  now on next to the baseline:

  | Rule set | What changes | July first delivery (after fees) | July second delivery (after fees) | November |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | baseline | nothing | 106 trades, +6.2 c | 58 trades, +6.4 c | 6 trades, -42.8 c |
  | `same_feed` | 5m and 15m only | 64 trades, +5.8 c | 32 trades, +8.4 c | 1 trade, +20 c |
  | `edge_8` | at least 8 c of edge | 73 trades, +9.5 c | 38 trades, +11.0 c | 3 trades, -33 c |

  On July the three cannot be told apart (standard errors of 4 to 8 cents). The baseline stays the
  rule of record. Which of the three is right for today's market is a question only our own
  recording can answer.
- All other variants were also positive on July's first delivery when held to expiry
  (`results/variants_july.csv`); the brief's original error bars gave 325 trades at +4.1 c before
  fees and +1.7 c after.

### Size

At the moment of each July tick trade the smaller of the two legs had a median of 53 shares at the
best price (middle half 23 to 141). Taking at most 200 shares per trade, the 164 trades made about
$1,100 after fees in 11 days; at most 50 shares, about $400. That is before our own orders move the
price. As it stands this is a small strategy.

### What this does and does not show

- **Shown, on July data:** contracts that expire together disagree by more than noise often enough
  to trade, the disagreement predicts the outcome, and the frozen rules earned about 6 cents per
  trade after fees on tick-level data, twice.
- **Not shown:** that any of this holds under today's settlement rule. Six trades cannot show it
  either way, and five of them lost.
- **Honest strength of the evidence:** two standard errors after fees on the best data, 1.5 with
  the polls, 1.1 with November. Several rule sets were looked at on the first delivery; only the
  baseline, `same_feed` and `edge_8` were judged on the second.
- **The test from here:** keep recording. At about 6 trades a day, 290 trades (enough to tell a
  5-cent edge after fees from zero) take about 7 weeks. Proposed decision points, to be agreed by
  the team: stop if after 100 trades the baseline's P&L after fees is more than two standard errors
  below +6 c (the July figure); call it confirmed only if after 290 trades it is two standard
  errors above zero. `python evidence.py summary` prints the running figures.
